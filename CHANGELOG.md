# Changelog

Notable changes to SimCruise, newest first. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/) and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/). Dates are release dates, not the survey dates of the data a release uses. The release notes written for Sunday Driver 1.0.0 to 1.0.8 are preserved word for word in [docs/history.md](docs/history.md).

## Unreleased

Both are back to the engine defaults (Reflex off); dynamic resolution stays. Bisected: sync alone 51 ms, Reflex alone 32 ms, neither 16.7 ms.
- **The pre-flight fails a spin, a run-off and slow frames; a replay goes to the place of its recording** (`Gameplay/SimCruisePreflight`, `Gameplay/SimCruiseInputTape`) — a run now FAILs when the rear axle slips past 15 deg or the car slides backwards (a spin), when it is off the road surface for more than 1 s or more than 2 m past the road's edge for more than 0.5 s (a run-off), and when frames are slow after the warm-up (95th percentile over 50 ms, or more than 5 frames over 100 ms, not counting the robot's own screenshots). The reasons are in the PREFLIGHT line, the report and the index row. `scripts/preflight.ps1 -ForceSpin` is the negative test: every run must fail with a spin. A recording (F9 or `-SimCruiseRecordInput`) now keeps the place of the drive and its world frame, and a replay travels there before it puts the car at the recorded start, so a Dragon tape replays on the Dragon, not on Ocean Drive. An older tape replays at the game's start with a warning.

The rpm next to the rev bar is the engine's own speed to the nearest 50 in every HUD level (idle, about 850 rpm, at a stop). The drive capture logs the HUD against the car (`DRIVECAPTURE HUD shows ...`), shoots the Settings page, and takes `-SimCruiseDriveCaptureThrottle=0..1` (default 0.45) for shots at speed.

Understeer gradient 3.1 to 0.8 deg/g; the Dragon pre-flight now stays on the road.
- **A keen driver for the road tests** (`scripts\preflight.ps1 -Keen`) — no speed limit, corners at 75 % of the car's own skidpad grip, brakes at 70 %, up to 45 m/s, looking 200 m ahead, traffic off: the owner's acceptance drive.
- **Pad record and replay** (developers) — `-SimCruiseRecordInput` or F9 records steer, throttle, brake, clutch, handbrake and gear changes with time, the car and the start pose; `-SimCruiseReplayInput=<file>` drives the recording again in any build and writes speed, yaw rate, slip and lap time to a CSV. `scripts\replay-compare.ps1` runs one recording on two builds or two cars and prints the differences. See `docs/unreal-building.md`, "Drive it".
- **Hot reload while the game runs** (developers) — save a car file, its calibration overlay, `CarPresentation.json`, the seat and lens in `preferences.json` or the exposure and colour grade in `Saved/SimCruise/look.json`, and about 1.5 s later the game uses it, with "Reloaded <file>" on screen and no rebuild. A car file respawns the car in place, at rest. Off in Shipping. See `docs/unreal-building.md`, "Hot reload".
- **Faster checks on a shared PC** (developers) — `scripts\viewcheck.ps1` takes the GPU lock once for all its cases instead of queueing again for each (a three-case check waited about 30 minutes on a busy PC), and `scripts\check.ps1` gives at least two headless test slots when 20 GB or more of memory is free.
- **A sharper, steadier picture** (Aaron: "the graphics look fuzzy and it looks like it was glitching / laggy") — the picture is sharpened after the TSR upscale, more when the internal picture is smaller (`r.Tonemapper.Sharpen` 0.4 at 100 %, 0.9 at Medium's 58 %); on Lummus Park the realism detail score rose 67.0 to 68.1 with no halos. Motion blur now follows the real frame rate like a camera shutter (the engine default blurred every frame as if the game ran at 30 fps) and is shorter (amount 0.3, at most 2 % of the screen). VSync is on by default (`simcruise.VSync`): with only a 60 fps cap on a 60 Hz laptop screen a tear line rolled through the picture. Measured cause of most of the lag on the owner's laptop: the GPU was held at 28 W (855 of 2100 MHz), about 42 fps on Medium in a packaged build; the laptop's Performance mode gives it its full power.
- **Fix: false "no road surface" reports in the pre-flight** — the probe traced only the tile the point lies in, so within a few centimetres of a tile border it read 1.3 m "gaps" on US 129 and CA-1. It now traces the neighbouring tiles too and takes the highest surface: 0 gaps on 4 runs.
- **Frame check at speed** (`scripts/framecheck.ps1`) — the pre-flight robot at a set speed (default 130 km/h), with no screenshots, on any preset and window size, in the editor or a packaged build (`-Exe`). It writes each frame's time and every stall over 50 ms (`PREFLIGHT FRAMES ... p50 p99 worst over33 over50`). Measure in a packaged build: the editor runs 2-3 times slower.
- **The 1965 Mustang's driver sits 6 cm higher** (`driver_eye`) — the eye was 0.97 m off the ground; the upright seat and 51 in roof put a real driver's eye near 1.03-1.05 m (owner's playtest: it sat too low).

## SimCruise 2.0.2 (2026-11-13)

A patch release after 2.0.1: a GPS that drives you on the real roads, a new pause menu and HUD, the seat and the car's setup one press away, more views, traffic you can turn down, and fixes found by the new pre-flight robot. Windows installer and zip as before. What to try first, in plain words: [docs/release/v2.0.2-what-you-should-notice.md](docs/release/v2.0.2-what-you-should-notice.md).

Known issues in 2.0.2: the dashboard gauges are not fixed (the speedometer needle and the other dials do not follow the car; read the speed on the HUD); a 1.3 m strip with no road surface in the hairpins of the Tail of the Dragon about 515 m from the start (P4) and, now and then, a strip, a step of up to 7 cm or a jolt at one tile border on CA-1, Big Sur (P5); on the Tail of the Dragon the trees beside the road can drop out for a frame and the cabin brightness can flicker (P6, older than 2.0.2); strong motion blur on near objects at 25-30 mph (P3); the Miata has no ABS, so a hard press of the brake trigger (about 60 %) locks the front wheels and the car cannot turn: press the trigger less to steer while braking (P7); a light throttle at low engine speed is weak, as in 2.0.1: press the trigger more to pull away (P8). Still open from 2.0.1: the Corvette C8's 0-60 (3.22 s against 2.8 s), the Mustang's drum-brake and launch spins, and the Miata's dash clock showing the start time. All bugs: [docs/release/bugs.md](docs/release/bugs.md).

GPS, menus and HUD:

- **GPS routes on the real roads** (`helper/route.mjs`, `Gameplay/SimCruiseRoute`, `Gameplay/SimCruiseRouteSubsystem`) — the map helper has a new `route` command. It builds a road graph from the same OpenStreetMap vector tiles it already reads (only the tiles round each leg's stops, from its cache) and finds the fastest way with A* on this computer, with no routing server: road class speeds, one-way streets, no tunnels (the game does not build them), street names, and turn steps (right, slight left, U-turn ...). In the game the car follows the route; more than 30 m off it for 3 s gives a new route from the car. At each stop "You are at <stop>" shows for 4 s, then the next leg starts.
- **The GPS on the HUD** — a green guide-sign turn panel at the top (the arrow, the distance in miles or in feet under 0.1 mi, "Right onto 5th Street", and "Then" with the turn after it), magenta chevrons on the road in the lane you drive, 5 to 60 m ahead, a magenta arrow board at the corner of the next turn, and a round map at the bottom right that turns with the car, with the magenta course, the amber stops, the distance and the arrival time.
- **A new look for the menus and the HUD** (`Gameplay/SimCruiseUiTheme`, `docs/ui/ui-spec.md`) — every menu row is a US green guide sign, easy to read from a sofa; the Overpass font (SIL OFL 1.1, in `data/fonts/overpass`). The speed is large at the bottom left with the gear in amber and a rev bar with its red zone; HUD levels Off, Glance and Full stay (F2, X). `simcruise.Hud.Look 0` brings the earlier HUD back.
- **Pause menu** (Menu or Tab) — four pages changed with LB and RB (Q and E): Drive (continue, Where to?, plan a route, famous roads, traffic, road atlas; the card on the right shows your route), Your car (car, gearbox, tune car, headlights), Seat & view (lens Natural / True FOV / Wide, head motion, screen size, eye distance; the world stays clear and runs behind it) and Settings (sound, HUD, rumble, stick and trigger settings). The settings keep their saved values. The road atlas is on the Drive page.
- **Plan a route** — a full-screen map with a cross: the left stick moves it, A adds a stop, X removes one, LT and RT zoom, Y starts the route, B goes back. Each stop shows the distance and time the router finds. Mouse and keyboard work too (click to add, drag, wheel, Enter, Delete).
- **Famous roads** — Ocean Drive (Miami Beach), the Tail of the Dragon (US 129), Big Sur (California 1) and Monument Valley (US 163): the game goes to the start and the route to the end starts when the road is ready.
- **"Where to?" can set a destination** — after you choose a found place: go there now, or make it your stop and drive there on the roads.
- The pause menu's Traffic row and Seat position row use the driving update's traffic switch and seat menu (same settings and saved values). The Tune car and seat screens keep their own look in 2.0.2.

Driving, seat and views:

- **Throttle-plate model, off for now** (``throttle_progression``, ``SimCruiseDynamics::EngineLoad``) — a real throttle plate gives most of the torque for a light pedal at low revs. It is in the car data model but set to 0 (the pedal is the load, as in 2.0.1): with the right trigger's curve as tuned, it made a 70 % trigger nearly full torque at launch revs and turning launches spun (drive robot). It returns with the trigger curve retuned to it.
- **Roll centres and the Torsen off the throttle** (`roll_centre_m`, `rear_diff_coast_bias`) — part of each axle's cornering load now reaches the outer tyre through the suspension links at once (NB 45 mm front, 90 mm rear, estimates) instead of all through the springs, and the NB's Torsen biases engine braking (1.5) as well as drive. Benches still on their published figures.
- **Look around and traffic off** — the right stick turns your head in the seat or on the hood (up to 110° each way, back to the road when you let go; its click stays the gearbox switch). The road atlas's Settings row has *Traffic* Normal, Light or Off, remembered.
- **Seat & view, and the car's menus one press away** — the menu has a *Your car* row (pad: D-pad to it, A): *Tune car...* opens the setup anywhere (saved on the move, it goes on at the next stop) and *Seat & view...* sets seat height, fore-aft, view tilt and view angle over the live driver's view, saved per car. The game keeps running behind the seat panel with the car on its brakes: a paused frame smeared in the temporal filters. A hint at the start of a drive says where the menus are. Cockpit settings have their own row, so none run off the screen.
- **Hood and far chase views** — C or Y cycles the seat, the hood, a near and a far chase and the scenic view. The view widens slightly with speed (6 % by 130 km/h).
- **Brake benches, from the expert review** — the brake-in-turn gate uses the deceleration the pedal asks for (a spin raised the measured one past the gate), the 70-0 threshold-braking driver holds a dead band about the peak slip instead of bang-bang, and BRAKESPLIT calls the sim's own valve (`SimCruiseDynamics::RearBrakePedal`).
- **Fix: the tuning menu's steering ratio no longer reads 0.0** — the car's setup was made before its model (which carries the real ratio) attached, so the row started at 0; an untuned ratio is now the real car's (15:1 on the Miata).
- **Fix: a car off the road no longer explodes** (`FSimCruiseVehicleSim::Step`) — wheel rays meeting near-vertical ground (a bank, a car on its side) drove the suspension into runaway loads: jolts of thousands of m/s², the speedometer at 160-800 mph, the 1965 Mustang tumbling off Tail of the Dragon and CA-1. A tyre now has no contact on ground more than 75° from the body's level, and a corner's load stops at ten times its static share. The Mustang's Tail of the Dragon pre-flight: off-road 37 s to 0, jolts 57 to 0.
- **Fix: a bad car-to-car contact can no longer fling the car** (`USimCruiseVehicleMovementComponent`) — a contact the solver resolved badly took a Miata from 21 to 249 mph over the forest on Tail of the Dragon. The car's speed may now rise by no more than 3 g and its spin by no more than 40 rad/s² (at most 12 rad/s), step after step, so a contact that keeps pushing for several frames is held too (a first guard stopped only the one-frame jump; a parked Corvette still reached 65 mph). Engines and tyres never give more than about 1.2 g, so normal driving is untouched; falling speed (a crash) is untouched; every clamp is logged.

World:

- **Grass you can see blades in** (the ground shader's GrassField branch) — lawns and verges from the land-cover map now have thin, tapering, leaning blades near the car, clumps and patches farther off, and a micro texture that does not repeat. Paths and roads stay clear. It costs about 1 ms of GPU on the High preset. `-SimCruiseNoGrassField` turns it off.
- **No baked sun shadows in the aerial photographs** — the bundled Miami Beach aerial photographs had the photographer's shadows in them, which disagreed with the game's own sun. The sun of each photograph is fitted from NOAA lidar heights and each shadow is lifted by the light ratio of its surface (0 ms of GPU). `-SimCruiseBakedAerial` keeps the originals.
- **Fix: a road step at tile borders** — when two neighbouring tiles were built at the same time, each lifted its seam end on its own, and the Corvette met a 9.9 cm step on CA-1 (Big Sur) in the pre-flight. Both sides now meet within 1 cm on an 8 % sidehill (`SimCruise.World.SlopedSeam`). A short road stub pinned at both ends now blends in one ramp instead of two steep ones.
- **Window frames, rails and poles stop flickering** — Temporal Super Resolution's thin-geometry detection is on (about 0.2 ms of GPU).

Tools (not in the game):

- **Pre-flight check before every play-test build** (`Gameplay/SimCruisePreflight`, `scripts/preflight.ps1`) — the robot drives Ocean Drive, the Tail of the Dragon, Princeton Road in Nebraska and Big Sur with the Miata, the Mustang and the Corvette, 60 s each, in the right lane of the real road near its speed limit. It records a video and a contact sheet of each drive, and fails on white sky, tiles or cars that appear near the camera, sudden picture changes, steps and gaps in the road, jolts at tile borders, stuck traffic, a start that falls back to another place, and a failed view check. `scripts/playtest.ps1` makes a play-test zip only for a commit whose pre-flight passed (`-SkipPreflight` with a warning).
- **Faster checks for several agents on one PC** (tools, not the game): `scripts\check.ps1` test runs no longer queue behind play tests and MCP sessions. They share up to 3 headless slots, and two full checks ran side by side. `-Tier fast` skips the slow tests (`Config\Tests\Tags.ini`), the build is skipped when no source changed, and every run is timed into `Saved\CheckTimes.csv`. Scripted game runs get a time limit and `-unattended`; `scripts\watchdog.ps1` lists stale runs.
- **View check: a test looks at the screen** (`Gameplay/SimCruiseViewCheck`, `scripts/viewcheck.ps1`) — a drive robot run showed the car on flat empty ground while the HUD named "Hotel Evans, Ocean Drive". That was the robot's test pad 60 m above the real street, not a world bug, but no test could see the difference. `-SimCruiseViewCheck` now traces a 48 x 27 grid of rays through the player's view, sorts the hits (road, building, ground, tree, car, props, other, sky), takes a screenshot with image statistics, and writes `VIEWCHECK PASS` or `FAIL` with the reasons. `scripts/check.ps1` runs it at Ocean Drive and Collins Avenue (must pass) and on the test pad (must fail), and keeps the screenshots in `Saved/ViewCheck/latest/`. On the test pad the HUD now shows an orange "DRIVE ROBOT · TEST PAD · NOT THE REAL ROAD" banner and no place cards.
- **The scene parser has open terms** (`Live/SimCruiseSceneParser`, `scripts/ai/fetch_open_models.py`) — the parser that finds the wall pixels in a building's photograph now runs the semantic head of Detectron2's Panoptic FPN (Apache 2.0, trained on COCO with CC BY 4.0 labels) instead of a Cityscapes model (non-commercial), which is removed. It tells building, house, roof, wall, window, sky, tree, grass, road, pavement and objects apart (windows and doors never count as wall) in 1.5-2.7 s on the CPU, and sees the sky better: on the Stardust Hotel photo the old model took a patch of blue sky for wall. Re-measured on the Miami Beach start tiles: 69 walls measured (62 before); the Regal Cinema is grey again and the Villa Casa Casuarina its cream stucco. The 120 MB model is a development tool fetched into `Saved/AI`, not committed and not shipped.
- **Realism score at more places** (`scripts\realism.ps1`, `lab/realism`) — the Tail of the Dragon and Bixby Bridge join the photograph poses, with game-and-photo side-by-sides.

## SimCruise 2.0.1 (2026-11-13)

A patch release after the first play of 2.0.0 ("field of views were off, too bright, traffic was bad, didn't look real"). Windows installer and zip as before.

What changed for the four points:

- **Too bright** — daylight is a third of a stop darker (night unchanged), and building walls now reflect like weathered paint (albedo caps, darkening-only stains, clipped photo pixels dropped from measured wall colours). Same spot on Ocean Drive, same time: the hotel fronts went from a mean of 156 to 137 (8-bit), the whole frame from 144 to 141; nothing clips.
- **Didn't look real** — the realism score at the Lummus Park photograph went from 34.3 to 35.5 out of 100 (tone 14.4 to 22.0); street furniture (benches, bins, bicycle racks, hydrants) from the map; the tile seams no longer lift whole road sections (they tilt, with the road's bank kept). Still far from a photograph: next phase.
- **Field of view** — 2.0.0 already starts on the natural lens; the lens is now on F6, or on the pad in the road atlas (Menu, D-pad to View, A). The left-stick click no longer changes it by accident.
- **Traffic** — not changed in 2.0.1; it is the first item of the next phase.

Also:

- **The AI models are no longer in the download** — the aerial-photo upscaler (Real-ESRGAN, trained on DIV2K) and the scene parser (DDRNet, trained on Cityscapes) were trained on data with research-only or non-commercial terms, so their weights stay development tools. The game runs without them: the aerial photographs are drawn without upscaling and the scene parser is off. `scripts\release.ps1` refuses a package with any `.onnx`.
- **Brakes and driving** — the Miatas' rear brakes no longer lock first (a real proportioning valve), a new brake-in-turn bench (ISO 7975) checks it, and engine braking includes pumping loss. Stock NB: 0-60 7.73 s, 70-0 177.9 ft, skidpad 0.891 g (published 7.8 s, 173 ft, 0.89 g).
- **Offline** — with no network at all the game starts on a road from the helper's cache and drives; the place graph never waits for the network.

Known issues in 2.0.1: the Corvette C8's 0-60 (3.22 s against 2.8 s), the Mustang's drum-brake and launch spins, traffic, the Miata's dash clock showing the start time instead of the time of day, and the tuning menu and stick settings were checked by tests, not by hand with a pad.

Everything else in 2.0.1:

- **The settings work with a pad** — in the road atlas, D-pad Left/Right picks a setting (it lights up), A changes it, B lets go; before, a pad could not reach them. The left-stick click no longer changes the lens (pressed by accident while steering). The stick dead-zone label shows the total, the fixed 12 % included.
- **The local AI reads photographs of the place** (`Live/SimCruiseSceneParser`, `data/ai/ddrnet23-slim-cityscapes.onnx`) — a second network runs on the CPU through Unreal's NNE: DDRNet23-Slim (MIT; Cityscapes weights, ONNX by Qualcomm AI Hub) labels every pixel of a street photograph as building, wall, sky, vegetation, road, car, pole, sign and so on, in 0.5-1 s. The place graph now measures a building's wall colour only on the pixels it labels building or wall, inside the wall's edge, without strongly blue pixels (sky the parser took for wall, or tinted glass), clipped highlights or deep shadow. An experimental `-AlbedoFromPavement` darkens over-exposed walls using the photograph's sidewalk or road as a grey card; it is off, because pavement brightness varied too much on the start tiles (known white walls came out grey); where the parser is not there the colour rule still works. On the Miami Beach start tiles the AI-masked colours agree with the colour rule within a few levels for most buildings and add three walls the rule could not measure (Fillmore Miami Beach, the Miami Beach Federal Savings and Loan building, SLS South Beach). The parser never runs on the game thread (the first try, on a commandlet's game thread, hung in ONNX Runtime); `ParseWithTimeout` gives up after 90 s and turns it off. `SimCruise.AI.Parse <image>` and `-SimCruiseParseDir=<folder>` write a labelled picture. `-run=SimCruisePlaceFacts -Photos -Remeasure` measures again. The parser's weights were trained on Cityscapes (non-commercial terms), so they stay a development tool: the packaged game and installer leave the model out. The same goes for the Real-ESRGAN weights of the AI detail engine (code and weights BSD-3, but trained on DIV2K, released for academic research only, with Flickr2K and OST): packaged builds leave them out and draw the original aerial photographs.
- **Your own stick and trigger settings** (road atlas, Controls row, saved) — stick steering speed (60-150 %), stick response (fine centre, between, linear), stick dead zone (0-15 %, full stick still reaches full lock) and the throttle trigger's curve (linear to very progressive). Defaults are unchanged. `SimCruise.Gameplay` checks the dead zone's rescaling.
- **Braking in a bend, tested** (`SimCruise.Vehicle.BrakeInTurn.*`, ISO 7975) — a new bench holds a steady 0.5 g on a 100 m circle, lifts and brakes at a fixed pedal from 0.3 to full with the wheel held, and fails if the car spins or (without ABS) a rear wheel locks before the fronts. It found every car spinning at mid pedal: the unloaded inside rear saturated and lost its side force. The Miatas' rear brakes now take 30 % of the braking (700 N m against 1600, from the caliper piston sizes; 36 % was too much), with the proportioning valve from 30 % pedal at half the rise above it: the rear share stays at or under the rear's share of the load from 0.15 to 0.8 g in a straight line (BRAKESPLIT lines), and the three Miatas stay in line at every pedal in the bend (peak sideslip 2-4°, fronts lock first). The 70-0 bench now threshold-brakes a car without ABS, as magazine testers do, instead of locking the fronts; with the braking grip back at its earlier values the stops are 176 / 178 / 159 ft against 173 / 173 / 160. The 1965 Mustang (drums, no valve) still spins at 0.8-1.0 pedal and is a stated open item. A spin counts only within 90 % of the car's skidpad grip.
- **Street furniture from the map** (`World/SimCruiseStreetFurniture`, `Knowledge/SimCruiseFurnitureData`) — benches, litter bins, bicycle racks, USPS boxes, fire hydrants, bollards, bus stops and shelters and picnic tables stand where OpenStreetMap maps them, at real size, behind the kerb; hydrants every 300 ft along curbed streets where the map has none. Colours follow the place graph (South Florida hydrants chrome yellow). 329 mapped items on the start tiles. `simcruise.World.Furniture`.
- **The place graph never holds up a frame and works with no network** — tile plans are shared instead of copied each half second, the open-data answers are parsed on a worker thread, a partial answer is not saved as final, and with no network the local AI turns itself off and the tiles use what is cached (play-tested with `-SimCruisePlaceOffline`: worst graph tick 0.47 ms).
- **Rear brakes no longer lock first on the Miatas** (`brake_rear_knee`, `brake_rear_slope`) — the NB's brake split sent 36 % to the rear at every pedal, so in a hard stop (front load share about 0.70) the rears locked first and the car swapped ends under heavy braking in a bend. A proportioning valve now passes full rear pressure up to 45 % pedal and 0.35 of the rise above it, so the fronts lock first as on the real car. Full-pedal capacity rises to 1600 N m front / 900 rear (about 80 bar in a panic stop): the old 1150 / 650 could not reach the grip of the stickier tyres once the valve cut the rear. Braking grip refitted: 70-0 in 173 / 173 / 159 ft against the published 173 / 173 / 160.

- **Tuning menu fixes** — the body-roll readout now uses the lowered centre of mass when you change ride height, and closing the menu after the car is gone no longer says the setup could not be saved.

## SimCruise 2.0.0 (2026-11-12)

The first full release of the Unreal Engine 5.8 client, for Windows: a Windows installer (per-user or all users, Start menu entry, uninstaller) and a portable zip, with SHA-256 checksums. Drive anywhere in the US in a 2001 Miata, a 1965 Mustang, a Corvette C8 and six other cars. The release gate (`docs/release/driving-checklist.md`, results in `docs/release/v2.0.0-report.md`) checked the cars against published road tests, the pad and wheel input, cameras, sound, traffic, crashes, frame rate and input delay on a laptop GPU, starting anywhere, settings, night driving, the cockpit field of view and a 20-minute drive in the Shipping build.

Known issues in 2.0.0:

- The Corvette C8 reaches 60 mph in 3.22 s (with a 1 ft rollout); Car and Driver measured 2.8 s. Its stop and skidpad match.
- The 1965 Mustang spins in a full-lock launch at 70 % trigger; the open differential should let the inside wheel spin instead.
- The Tesla Model 3 can spin when it trail-brakes hard at 0.7 g: the game has no stability-control model yet.
- An offline start was tested with the live feeds blocked (`-SimCruiseLiveOffline`), not with the whole network off.
- On an unlit rural road at night the low beams light the road to about 45-50 m.
- Fields in the Great Plains are bright green in November instead of the brown of harvested crops.
- The tuning menu (D-pad right / F4) was tested by its model test and by loading a saved setup in the Shipping build, not by hand by the release gate.

Release gate fixes:

- **A new pad layout** — the clutch is on A (hold), the parking brake on B (hold), the gears on the bumpers (RB up, LB down), the gearbox switch on the right-stick click, the engine restart on D-pad left and the HUD on X. The clutch was on LB, the same finger as the brake trigger, and hard steering pressed the left stick (the HUD) by accident. D-pad right opens the tuning menu.
- **Steady head motion and g-meter** (`FSimCruiseHeadModel`, `FSimCruiseGMeter`) — they divided the velocity change by the frame time while the 360 Hz physics gives 5, 6 or 7 new steps in a 60 fps frame, so they jittered by about 17 %. They now use the physics time (`SimCruise.Gameplay.Camera.PhysicsTimeNoJitter`).
- **No camera swoop after a recover** — the reset happens on the physics thread a frame or more after the request, so the snap came too early; the camera now snaps on the frame the car is seen to jump or turn upright.
- **Frame pacing** — a 60 fps cap (`simcruise.FrameCap`, 0 for none), and the RTX 2070 Super Max-Q and other Max-Q laptop GPUs start on the Medium preset: in the Shipping build at 1080p, Ocean Drive from the driver's seat ran 57 fps (1 % low 44) on High and 77 fps (1 % low 61) on Medium. NVIDIA Reflex low latency is available (`simcruise.Reflex 1`) but off by default: with it on, the Shipping game hung at exit; it is now turned off before the engine exits.
- **Crash bench** (`SimCruise.Vehicle.Crash.<car>`) — each release car hits a wall at 60 km/h, a tree at 50 km/h, a guard rail at 80 km/h and a stopped car at 50 km/h: no launch, no tunnelling, no NaN, and the reset drives on.
- **Release tools** — `-SimCruiseLatencyProbe` (stick to picture), `-SimCruiseSoak=<minutes>` (an unattended drive with crashes, recovers, camera, car and place changes), `scripts\release.ps1` (Shipping package, map helper, installer, checksums and smoke test in one step), `docs/release/RELEASE-CHECKLIST.md` for every release, and the drive robot now drives the Mustang and the Corvette too.
- **The map helper is built with the package** (`scripts/package.ps1` builds `tools/atlas.exe` from `helper/atlas.mjs` when Bun is installed), so a package never ships an older helper.

They stop where a crossing road runs through, and give way to measured paint (`PaintEvidence`, `MeasuredSection`, `RoadLayout`, `RoadSurvey`). `checks/LaneMarkingCheck.gd` (default suite) tests the rules on fixed cross-sections and a stripe over a synthetic 8.9° crest and a 0.5 m step, paints two bundled tiles again on a synthetic road ribbon (crests, superelevation, a width taper, overlapping ribbons at every junction and a mapped 2 + TWLTL + 2 count; the bundled tiles alone have no ribbon), checks that the lane counts are fetched for the whole ring nearest first, and fails on any script error raised while painting (`checks/ScriptErrorGuard.gd`, a `Logger`: a runtime error only abandons one road, so the check used to print PASSED over one), then builds tiles and samples every stripe triangle (corners, edge midpoints, centre) against the tile's colliders: the paint must sit 1–10 cm above the ribbon trimesh (or the nearest collider off it); places where the ground or a kerb collider rises above the ribbon, or the road surface itself folds more than 3 cm inside one piece, are counted as road-surface defects. With `--dir` it also reports marking time, height lookups and town/rural changes per tile; `lab/check-lane-counts.mjs` (helper suite) tests the tag parsing, matching (including one-way direction) and the cache-only read offline, and that every one-way width of `road-widths.mjs` is painted with two lanes and every ramp with one. Every ribbon is painted, whatever its class: `road-grade.mjs` chains a local street into the major road it continues as and names the chain after its first piece, and those major roads used to be painted along the map line beside their ribbon; each station is matched to its road by its own paved width. Marking cost, measured with `LaneMarkingCheck --dir` against the same tree with only the per-station width fix, three alternating runs each on a shared machine: Salida CO (9 tiles) 16.6 / 19.0 / 14.8 % of tile build → 7.8 / 7.5 / 7.3 % (a later pair under heavier load from other jobs: 16.6 → 9.5 % and 14.8 → 7.6 %; marking time 2.2–4.4× lower in every pair), the US-50 tile at Monarch Pass 52 → 26 ms (20 → 12 %); sparse single tiles with many lines still reach 12–18 %. The time went to height lookups where another ribbon was assumed near (now tested against the bounding boxes of the other ribbons' quads), quads drawn vertex by vertex (now one triangle fan), corner height lookups on a single ribbon (now flat across the 15 cm stripe), and stations on straight, uniform stretches, which are now left out where every line stays within 5 mm of its chord. `RoadSurface.height()` rejects quads by their bounding box first. `checks/RailView.gd --rail-car` frames the car and adds a driver's-eye `road-ahead.jpg` and a high `road-above.jpg`.

- **Tyres that peak and slide** (`Powertrain.gd`, `CarSpec.gd`) — longitudinal grip now follows a slip-ratio curve: full grip to the peak slip (0.10), falling toward a locked or spinning wheel by `slip_ratio_falloff`, so a spinning tyre also loses side grip; side-force relaxation length grows with load. The five modern cars (radials, ABS) fall by 10 % (Wong's 83–94 % sliding/peak on dry asphalt); the 1999 roadster and the 1965 Mustang stay flat as before, with their original parameters (the roadster's 0.963 peak and 1.105 lateral scale: a 1.07 peak sliding to 0.963 had raised its trail-braking grip 5.3 % without a measurement; the Mustang's estimated bias-ply curve made its full-throttle 0–60 10.7 s against 8.9 s). Traction control holds the most-slipping driven wheel at the same peak slip: the slip is read through a 20 ms filter and a slew limit, proportional 0.15 plus integral 3/s, no wind-up while the clutch slips at capacity, held through gear changes, torque cut off within 50 ms and restored over 0.15 s. ## SimCruise 1.1.0 (2026-11-09)

Renamed from Sunday Driver. The work below follows the 8 November 2026 design-panel report kept as [docs/roadmap.md](docs/roadmap.md).

### Changes

- **Controls** — speed-aware cap on keyboard and pad steering (the rack still allowed 18.8° at 45 mph where about 3° is usable), slower pedal and steering ramps, a keyboard-tap bench, and one-press recovery that notices a roof-down or stuck car, prompts for R and searches every road, not only main roads.
- **Mountain roads** — streamed roads are now built as road benches: a smooth profile measured along the curving road, a flat shelf with superelevation in rural curves, and cut and fill slopes that meet the hillside, instead of a terrain grid that skipped steep side slopes and capped corrections at 1.5 m.

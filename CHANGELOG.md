# Changelog

Notable changes to SimCruise, newest first. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/) and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/). Dates are release dates, not the survey dates of the data a release uses. The release notes written for Sunday Driver 1.0.0 to 1.0.8 are preserved word for word in [docs/history.md](docs/history.md).

## Unreleased

- **Road markings from the mapped lane counts** (`LaneMarkings.gd`, `helper/lane-counts.mjs`) — the field test of 2026-11-10 found Moab's five-lane Main Street (US-191) drawn as a two-lane road with a double yellow line, Kearney's US-30 with no markings, and edge lines missing almost everywhere, because every marking was guessed from the road class. The helper now reads the OpenStreetMap `lanes`, `lanes:forward`, `lanes:backward`, `oneway` and `maxspeed` tags of the major roads around each tile from Esri's OSM North America highways replica (one query per 2 × 2 tile group, cached 28 days; `--without-lane-counts` turns it off) and matches them to every segment of the tile's roads (tile JSON `road_lanes`, matched only to parallel ways within 6 m: two-way to two-way, and one-way only to a one-way way running the same direction, so a one-way street never takes the opposite carriageway or a side street's mouth; by majority vote along the segment). The query is off the first tile's critical path: the tile command answers from the cache at once (`road_lanes` status `pending` when nothing is cached), and `BuildingStream.gd` fetches the counts for the tile under and ahead of the car in the background (helper `lane-counts` command, road rows passed in a cache file) and repaints the markings; `--wait-lane-counts` makes the tile command wait, for tools and field tests. The game paints them after MUTCD Part 3: white edge lines (6 in on arterials) on motorways, trunks and primaries everywhere and on collectors outside towns; a yellow left edge on one-way carriageways; a double yellow centre line on two-way roads with two or more lanes each way or in town; on rural two-lane roads a broken yellow line, solid on the side without the Table 3B-1 passing sight distance (measured over the road surface with a 3.5 ft eye and object, zones under 400 ft removed); a two-way left-turn lane (solid outside, broken inside) only where OSM counts a lane in neither direction (`lanes` = forward + backward + 1, OSM's `lanes:both_ways`, e.g. 5 = 2 + 1 + 2) or gives an odd total with no split, and, in town, for a 2 + 1 or 3 + 2 split (the left-turn pocket of a junction approach). Outside towns such a split stays as mapped: a climbing or passing lane, with the two-lane side solid and the single lane broken where it has the passing sight distance; 10 ft white lane lines on a world-anchored 40 ft cycle between lanes in the same direction; no centre line on a single lane or under 16 ft; nothing on local streets. Without a mapped count a segment takes the counts of the same road within 250 m, then the counts mapped on most of the same street in the tile (symmetric splits only), then the US default: one lane each way, and one lane on a one-way road unless it is an arterial (the road width is itself a class estimate). Lanes are never narrower than 2.75 m: a count the road cannot hold drops the centre lane, then lanes from the busier direction. Town or rural (edge lines on collectors, passing zones) follows a mapped speed limit, else the share of the street's length in the tile that is built up, with a majority over 150 m each way on mixed streets, so it no longer switches from block to block; the built-up count is scaled up near the tile edge. Lines follow the road ribbon's 2 m stations and sit 4.8 cm above the surface the tyres drive on (the ribbon's own cross-sections, or `height_at()` where ribbons overlap or there is none); a quad ends where the surface leaves its chord by 1.5 cm (crests and sags), a step between two points is halved down to 25 cm and cut out if it is a real step, a piece across a step in the road surface is left out, and each tile paints only inside its own square. They stop where a crossing road runs through, and give way to measured paint (`PaintEvidence`, `MeasuredSection`, `RoadLayout`, `RoadSurvey`). `checks/LaneMarkingCheck.gd` (default suite) tests the rules on fixed cross-sections and a stripe over a synthetic 8.9° crest and a 0.5 m step, then builds tiles and samples every stripe triangle (corners, edge midpoints, centre) against the tile's colliders: the paint must sit 1–10 cm above the ribbon trimesh (or the nearest collider off it); places where the ground or a kerb collider rises above the ribbon, or the road surface itself folds more than 3 cm inside one piece, are counted as road-surface defects. With `--dir` it also reports marking time, height lookups and town/rural changes per tile; `lab/check-lane-counts.mjs` (helper suite) tests the tag parsing, matching (including one-way direction) and the cache-only read offline. `checks/RailView.gd --rail-car` frames the car and adds a driver's-eye `road-ahead.jpg` and a high `road-above.jpg`.
- **`scripts/check.sh` can no longer pass a check that never ran** — on Windows (Git Bash) the perl time limit could not start the Godot `.exe`, so every check left an empty log and still printed `pass`. The script now uses GNU `timeout` where it exists (Linux, Git Bash) and perl's alarm otherwise (stock macOS), and any check whose log is empty fails with exit 127. Four new checks joined the default suite today: `LiftOff`, `TractionControlCheck`, `CombinedSlip` and `AbsReverse` (see [docs/cars.md](docs/cars.md)).
- **The 1999 roadster no longer spins on a light lift or trail brake** — the field test's Miata spins (Tail of the Dragon, Storm King NY-218, Leakey RR 337, one rollover) came from the automatic gearbox, not the chassis: a brake input below the 0.01 brake threshold while rolling forward selected reverse, and reverse stayed engaged while the brake was held, so the clutch drove the rear wheels backwards mid-corner (RoadDrive trace at the Dragon's 1,151 m: rear slip ratio −1.25 at 18 m/s, gear R). Every car did it (108–1,186 ticks on one Dragon run); the light rear-drive Miata spun. `Powertrain.gd` now changes direction only below 0.45 m/s, like a real selector's reverse inhibitor, and a pedal too light to brake coasts. Dragon forward: impact, 3.7 g and a car stuck off the road → clean; NY-218 reverse: 7.3 m off the road → clean; the Leakey rollover run (11.4 km) now finishes, its remaining jolts all inside the known broken-surface zone. Track, CarTrack (all seven cars), SBend and RoadProfile give the same numbers to the last digit. **`checks/LiftOff.gd`** (new, default suite) holds every car on a 40 m circle at 0.3/0.5/0.7 g and lifts off or trail-brakes at 0.15 g with the wheel frozen (after ISO 9816 / ISO 7975): no spin at 0.3–0.5 g, and no reverse at speed; before the fix the Miata spun at 0.5 g (45° sideslip) and every car but the Mustang selected reverse. Brake bias, differential, toe, camber gain, load sensitivity, inertia and centre-of-gravity experiments moved the NB's 0.7 g response by at most 0.11 in yaw-rate ratio, so no chassis value changed. `RoadDrive.gd` sets the car down at its ride height instead of dropping it 0.35 m (that drop and the reverse wheel hop were the Miata's "bump-stop ticks on smooth roads": 4–8 → 0 on the Dragon and NY-218), prints where any bump stop is reached, and `ROADDRIVE_WHEELS=1` adds each tyre's load, slip angle, slip ratio and the gear to its trace. See `docs/cars.md`.
- **Tyres that peak and slide** (`Powertrain.gd`, `CarSpec.gd`) — longitudinal grip now follows a slip-ratio curve: full grip to the peak slip (0.10), falling toward a locked or spinning wheel by `slip_ratio_falloff`, so a spinning tyre also loses side grip; side-force relaxation length grows with load. The five modern cars (radials, ABS) fall by 10 % (Wong's 83–94 % sliding/peak on dry asphalt); the 1999 roadster and the 1965 Mustang stay flat as before, with their original parameters (the roadster's 0.963 peak and 1.105 lateral scale: a 1.07 peak sliding to 0.963 had raised its trail-braking grip 5.3 % without a measurement; the Mustang's estimated bias-ply curve made its full-throttle 0–60 10.7 s against 8.9 s). ABS (`abs_enabled`, renamed from `abs`, which shadowed the built-in) modulates each wheel about its peak slip in either direction of travel. Traction control holds the most-slipping driven wheel at the same peak slip: the slip is read through a 20 ms filter and a slew limit, proportional 0.15 plus integral 3/s, no wind-up while the clutch slips at capacity, held through gear changes, torque cut off within 50 ms and restored over 0.15 s. Clutch coupling is limited to what closes the speed gap in one substep (it overshot and alternated sign at lock-up: on main for 2.2 s in the Kestrel S launch and 1.75 s in the Corvette's), and a dual clutch shifts without interrupting the drive. `CarTrack.gd` drives every car with the same full pedal again (no bench test driver). New default checks: `TractionControlCheck` (fails on sustained torque or driveline oscillation in a full-throttle launch or power-on corner exit; the first version of this branch failed it on four cars), `CombinedSlip` (braking while cornering against main; fails on a gain over 3 % for a car without ABS) and `AbsReverse` (ABS stops forwards and in reverse). CarTrack, main → now: roadster 7.95 → 7.99 s, 172.9 → 172.9 ft; Kestrel S 6.83 → 6.92 s (automatic launch at 2500 rpm instead of 4000), 161.8 → 162.8 ft; Tora RS 5.58 → 5.61 s, 154.4 → 155.5 ft; Volta 5.30 → 5.35 s, 166.3 → 167.0 ft; Corvette 3.60 → 3.55 s (launch at 3000 rpm instead of 4000; still a stated open item against 2.8 s), 149.1 → 152.0 ft; Mustang 9.00 → 9.00 s, 258.9 → 259.3 ft; Bison XL 5.68 → 5.73 s, 188.8 → 189.4 ft: all inside their bands except the Corvette's pre-existing 0–60 item. `Track.gd`: 7.989 s, 172.9 ft, 0.8846 g (main 7.948 s, 173.0 ft, 0.8846 g); `LockedBrakeTurn` 27.67 m (27.67).
- **Review fixes for the traffic director and mapped rails** — signal stop bars now sit behind the widest road through the junction (its half width plus a 3 m crosswalk) instead of a fixed 7 m before the node, so cars no longer stop with their nose in a wide cross street; the local-LLM traffic character and the mapped-rail Overpass fetch are opt-in; the Overpass query's server timeout matches the client's 3 s budget. Open from review: skewed-junction phase assignment, late SignalControls matches, oncoming check when passing a parked van, guard-rail behaviour on real OSM data (every Overpass mirror was down while testing).
- **Houses with eaves, doors and garages** (`HouseRecipe.gd`) — detached houses are no longer flush-roofed boxes. Roofs overhang the walls by 0.3–0.5 m (per house), keeping their pitch over the rakes, closed by a soffit and a fascia board, with gutters on the eaves and downspouts on eaves of 6 m or more. The wall that faces the nearest street gets a front door (one of five common door colours) in a trim frame with a concrete stoop (a second step where the lot falls away); footprints of 120 m² or more get an attached 9 ft or 16 ft garage door on the same wall about three times in four, NSI duplexes get two doors, about two houses in five get a shed porch roof on posts and about three pitched roofs in ten a brick chimney. Ground-floor windows make room for the doors. Every choice comes from the footprint, height, NSI use and the street network plus a per-building hash, so it works on any US house and is the same on every machine; colliders are unchanged. Cost on the Lincoln, NE suburb start (1,562 houses): 89.7 → 165.4 triangles per house. `--plain-houses` restores the old boxes for A/B shots, and `checks/RailView.gd` now also frames the nearest house front (`house-front.jpg`, `house-oblique.jpg`) and reports triangles per house.
- **Traffic that drives like people** (`TrafficDirector.gd`) — ambient cars now use the Intelligent Driver Model (Treiber, Hennecke & Helbing 2000: desired speed from the road class, time headway, standstill gap, comfortable acceleration and braking, ballistic update so a long frame cannot roll a car through its leader) instead of a two-second speed rule. A low-rate director gives each car a persona — commuter, delivery van (drawn as a pickup), cautious elder, hurried driver, tourist — with its own IDM parameters varied ±10 % per driver, and about once a second plans its route through the next junctions (turn choice by persona: bigger roads and straight on for commuters and hurried drivers, residential streets for vans, wandering tourists), the cornering speed to slow to before a sharp turn, stops at mapped signals (SignalControls matches and OSM signal nodes, fixed-time two-phase plans with a yellow dilemma decided by comfortable braking) and the occasional kerbside stop of a delivery van, which following cars ease round. The persona mix follows the place: road classes and mapped POI classes within 800 m give a traffic character by rule; with `--traffic-llm` (off by default: a loaded model costs gigabytes of RAM and CPU time while driving) and a local Ollama server answering (`OLLAMA_HOST`, CPU only, first of qwen3:1.7b / qwen3:4b (Apache-2.0), phi4-mini (MIT), granite4:tiny-h (Apache-2.0) installed), it is asked once per z14 area for the same character as one-word JSON, asynchronously with a 2 s timeout, and the rule-based answer stands when it is absent or late (`--without-traffic-llm`, `--traffic-llm-model=`, `--traffic-llm-timeout=`). Car heights are now sampled from the road ribbon every 6 m and interpolated, which took AmbientLife at the Cheyenne capitol from 2.36 ms to 0.8–1.15 ms a frame (32 cars, Ultra). `checks/TrafficCheck.gd` (default suite): every persona stopping behind a stopped car from 5–33 m/s at 60 Hz and 10 Hz rests 1.4–3.1 m behind it and never closer than 1.36 m; a 9-car mixed platoon behind a leader braking at 6 m/s² never closes below 3.48 m; signal phases never conflict. `--rail-view` also saves `traffic.jpg` / `traffic-signal.jpg` and prints traffic cost and behaviour (`--rail-wait=`).
- **Mapped guard rails** (`helper/guard-rails.mjs`, `GuardRails.gd`) — OSM `barrier=guard_rail` and `barrier=jersey_barrier` ways are the preferred source when you opt in with `--mapped-guard-rails` (off by default, so a default install sends nothing to the volunteer-run public Overpass service; the project's earlier zero-requests policy stands): the helper then fetches them at runtime for each z14 area (one query, cached 90 days, never bundled: ODbL) from a rotating list of public Overpass mirrors with exponential backoff inside a 3 s budget, treats partial answers as failures, and keeps a negative cache per area plus a provider-wide pause, so a saturated Overpass costs one short attempt and then nothing (Tail of the Dragon tile with a warm cache: 3.7 s on the first, failing, attempt, then 0.8–1.1 s against 1.0–1.4 s with the lookup off). Tiles carry them as `guard_rails` rows in tile units. Each mapped way is fitted to the road ribbon it runs along: points within 6 m of that shoulder move onto it at the shoulder height, farther points blend back to their mapped position, and nothing is left on a carriageway. W-beam faces the traffic (inferred rails on the left of travel now do too); jersey barriers are a concrete F-shape, 810 mm. Inferred rails give way within 15 m of a mapped one. Every public mirror was still saturated today, so the mapped path was verified with hand-written synthetic Overpass-shaped data (`lab/check-guard-rails.mjs`, in `scripts/check-helper.sh`; and an uncommitted fixture for a Tail of the Dragon capture: 500 m mapped, 271 m of inferred rail suppressed). `SIMCRUISE_GUARD_RAILS=offline` keeps to the cache; `checks/RailView.gd` prefers framing a mapped rail and prints mapped and inferred lengths.
- **The 1999 roadster against photographs** — `checks/CarTurntable.gd` (`--car-turntable DIR`, windowed, with `--quality=ultra`) photographs the car exactly as `CarFinish.gd` builds it on a neutral pad from eight angles, the top, close-ups and the driver view, with `--lamps` adding dusk shots. Compared with Commons photographs of 1999 cars (`lab/miata/appearance/turntable`), `CarFinish.gd` now gives the hardtop and glass shared normals (they were loose flat quads that reflected the sky as tiles), a lighter green tint to the glass (34 % instead of 58 % opaque), black trim, underbody and wheel-arch liners instead of light blue-grey, a body-colour windscreen frame at half the tube radius, a black cowl grille, larger mirrors moved to the front corner of the doors, and darker metallic factory-silver wheels. From the seat the hood is green rather than chrome and a hardtop lining closes the open-topped cabin. **L** switches the head lamps: the lenses glow, two low-beam spot lights light the road, the amber markers glow, and the stop lamps keep a 0.42 tail glow under their 1.72 braking glow. Physics, mounts and collision are unchanged.
- **Houses that read as houses** — `HouseWall.gdshader` gives detached houses world-space lap siding (6.5 in boards with a shadow line under each lap and a downward-tilted face) or US modular brick in running bond, from a palette of 12 common US exterior paints and 4 brick colours chosen per house (about one in four brick), instead of four flat beige-greys. `SurveyRoof.gdshader` draws 3-tab asphalt shingle courses (5 5/8 in exposure, offset cut-outs, granule variation) over the observed aerial roof colour on every pitched roof, taking the facet slope from the geometry so every roof builder gets it; the plain fallback roof uses the same shader. Detail fades into its average shade once a board, course or brick is below a few pixels, so distant houses neither shimmer nor change tone. Window glass reflects instead of being a flat panel. `checks/RailView.gd` now uses its own camera and adds an oblique aerial view.
- **Ground without repeats** (`Stochastic.gdshaderinc`) — grass, leaf litter, grit, mineral ground, road wear and asphalt micro-normals no longer repeat at fixed periods: a hex grid of cells each reads the texture at its own offset (and rotation where the texture has no direction), blended so the texture's mean and contrast are preserved (Heitz & Neyret 2018; Mikkelsen 2022, written from the papers). +0.26 ms GPU at Ultra on the bundled Miami Beach start (8.10 → 8.36 ms). First slice of the detail-engine plan in `docs/research/detail-engine.md` (algorithmic surfaces in the hot path, local AI only offline or at the menu).
- **A driven car** — `CarPaint.gdshader` gives the 1999 roadster metallic paint with mica flakes that glint at the sun and a millimetre-scale clearcoat orange peel, both fading out below a pixel so nothing shimmers. `RoadGrime.gdshader` (as `material_overlay`, so it works on every car, including downloaded models) draws road film on the sills, spray behind each wheel and brake dust on the wheels; `CarWear.gd` grows it with distance (0.002/km paved, 0.05 gravel, 0.12 off-road), washes it off while the live weather station reports precipitation (new `wet` flag from the METAR present-weather string in `helper/conditions.mjs`) and remembers it (preference `car_dirt`, new players start at 0.15). Scripted captures keep the car clean. `--car-dirt=` sets it for A/B shots.
- **Fresher reflections** — the car's reflection probe now re-renders every 12 m on High and 8 m on Ultra (was 32 m).
- **Forward+ lighting hooks** — when the game runs on the Forward+ renderer, High and Ultra enable SSAO, SSIL, volumetric fog, glow and FSR 2, and Ultra adds SDFGI (`GraphicsQuality.cinematic_lighting`). Not the default renderer yet: on the bundled Miami Beach start it costs 14.7 ms (High) / 17.0 ms (Ultra) of GPU against 7–8 ms on Mobile, for a modest visible gain; the realism gap is mostly in textures and content.
- **Engineered road surface** (`RoadSurface.gd`, `helper/road-grade.mjs`) — every paved road on a streamed tile is now driven on, and drawn as, a ribbon built from the helper's fitted road profile (a station every 2 m with height, superelevation and a smoothed direction) instead of the 3.9 m terrain triangles, whose facets on banked mountain curves were felt as kinks. The ribbon uses the ground's own material, has 1.2 m shoulders, meets intersecting roads by rank (the minor road joins the major road's surface, fading over 15 m), and `height_at()` follows it; ground under it sits 6 cm below. Over 18 km of the Tail of the Dragon (US 129) the tyre-contact surface went from 34.6 to 6.4 slope kinks per km above 0.5°/m, the sharpest from 3.93°/m to 1.14°/m, and the tyre's vertical speed at 45 mph from 1.38 to 0.40 m/s (`checks/RoadProfile.gd`, which now measures the highest collider). `RoadDrive` at 0.4 g finished the reverse direction with a 0.23 g peak vertical load (0.30 g on the old surface). `--grid-roads` keeps the old surface.
- **Guard rails** (`GuardRails.gd`) — placed where a highway engineer would: wherever the ground falls more than 3 m within 8 m of a road ribbon's shoulder (steeper than the 1:3 slope the AASHTO Roadside Design Guide treats as non-recoverable), extended 8 m past each hazard. Galvanised W-beam at the US standard 0.79 m height on posts every 1.905 m, with a collider. On the Tail of the Dragon this puts about 8.5 km of rail on 18 km of road, on the drop-off sides. Mapped OSM rails were the first choice, but every public Overpass server was saturated or timing out; inference works on any road, mapped or not. `--without-guard-rails` turns them off; `checks/RailView.gd` (`--rail-view DIR`) captures them.
- **Checks keep scripts intact** — the editor import in `scripts/check.sh` re-saved some scripts with "convert indent on save", turning 4-space indentation into tabs (Drive.gd, BuildingStream.gd), so any check that loads the game failed to compile. The script now restores every `.gd` the import rewrote.
- **S-bend bench** (`checks/SBend.gd`, default suite) — a lift-off left-right transition at 40 mph across rack amplitudes; the 1999 car tracks it without spinning to 0.81 g (3.1° peak sideslip), as the real car does.
- **Driver lens** (`ViewLens.gd`) — the in-car view defaults to a natural 45° vertical field of view instead of 67° (which stretched distances and exaggerated speed); F6 cycles natural, true-to-screen (from the display's physical size and a 60 cm viewing distance) and wide. Scripted captures keep 67°.
- **Estimated road roughness off** — `RoadRoughness.gd` stays in the tree but runs only with `--rough-roads`, until measured roughness (HPMS IRI) replaces its class estimate.
- **Graphics presets** (`GraphicsQuality.gd`) — Low, Medium, High and Ultra, chosen automatically from the GPU the renderer opened (High on an RTX 2070 Super Max-Q, Low on integrated graphics), switched with **F4** and remembered. Mesh detail coarsens with speed, and on Low to High the 3D resolution follows measured GPU time, so dense towns drop resolution, not frames. Headless and QA runs keep `project.godot` as it was. `checks/QualityBench.gd` (`--quality-bench DIR`) measures each preset and saves captures.
- **Moving traffic and birds** (`AmbientLife.gd`) — cars drive the mapped streets in the right-hand lane at class speeds, follow at two-second gaps, stop behind the player, turn at junctions and never appear close in plain view; the six nearest are solid. Each model is one MultiMesh (about 0.5–1 ms for 20 cars). Flocks of birds are animated entirely in a vertex shader.
- **Road roughness** (`RoadRoughness.gd`) — each tyre rides a road profile sized by IRI from road class, per-street condition and a regional field (2.5 mm RMS on a new motorway, 6.5 mm on a typical street, 16 mm on gravel), applied to suspension travel and damper speed, never to the collider. `--smooth-roads` turns it off.
- **Cars from Sketchfab** — `authoring/cars/sketchfab.mjs` searches Sketchfab, fetches CC0 or CC BY models with your own API token, refuses other licences and models named after a car maker, and records credits in `game/art/traffic/manifest.json` and `licenses/Traffic models.txt`. Traffic loads them, normalised to their stated length.
- **Real cars** — a 1965 Mustang 289 four-speed (new, calibrated to Car Life and Road & Track: 0–60 mph 9.00 s, 70–0 mph 259 ft, skidpad 0.70 g estimated) and the Corvette Stingray Z51 (C8) on its existing calibrated physics, both with CC BY models from Sketchfab fitted automatically by `ModelRig.gd`; both also join the moving traffic.
- **`checks/AmbientCheck.gd`** (default suite) — GPU tier detection, model normalisation and roughness bands.
- **`docs/research/real-world-detail.md`** — where the remaining realism comes from (house recipes from ACS, parcel and NSI data, NBI bridges, HPMS roughness, impostors, VRS) and the licence findings behind each source.

## SimCruise 1.1.0 (2026-11-09)

Renamed from Sunday Driver. The work below follows the 8 November 2026 design-panel report kept as [docs/roadmap.md](docs/roadmap.md).

### Changes

- **Controls** — speed-aware cap on keyboard and pad steering (the rack still allowed 18.8° at 45 mph where about 3° is usable), slower pedal and steering ramps, a keyboard-tap bench, and one-press recovery that notices a roof-down or stuck car, prompts for R and searches every road, not only main roads.
- **Mountain roads** — streamed roads are now built as road benches: a smooth profile measured along the curving road, a flat shelf with superelevation in rural curves, and cut and fill slopes that meet the hillside, instead of a terrain grid that skipped steep side slopes and capped corrections at 1.5 m. Streamed tiles use a 128 grid drawn adaptively, so only cells on the bench gain triangles. On the first 7.7 km of the Tail of the Dragon (US 129, Deals Gap) the RMS slope change under the tyres fell from 1.19° to 0.20° and the worst from 21.6°/m to 3.3°/m; the scripted test drive (`checks/RoadDrive.gd`, new) rolled the car within 0.7 km before and now finishes both directions without leaving the ground. `checks/RoadProfile.gd` gains `--dir=` and `--classes=` to measure any streamed tiles.
- **Steep ground** — on slopes above about 30° the ground shader blends from the downward-projected aerial photograph (which smears down the hill) to rock and soil sampled triplanar in world space, keeping the photograph's broad local colour, with faint strata from world height. Road cuts at the Tail of the Dragon now read as rock instead of a streaked photo.
- **Cars as data, and five new cars** — every number the physics uses moved into a car file (`game/cars/*.json`, read by `CarSpec.gd`); `CarSuspension.gd` generalises the NB suspension and limited-slip maths, and `Powertrain.gd` gains front, all-wheel and electric drive. The 1999 roadster loads from its own file and `Track.gd` reproduces 7.94811604484319 s, 172.999178885236 ft and 0.88464155625327 g to the last digit. New cars with real, sourced specifications and made-up names: Kestrel S (front-drive compact), Tora RS (all-wheel-drive sedan), Volta (electric sedan), Meridian Z (mid-engine V8) and Bison XL (4WD pickup). `checks/CarTrack.gd` (new, in the default suite) holds each to published 0–60 mph, 70–0 mph and skidpad figures; five of six are inside their bands, the Meridian's 0–60 is a stated open item. Each new car has a model generated in Blender by `authoring/cars/build_car.py` (lofted body, projected glass, lamps and grilles, spoked wheels with discs and calipers, a cabin you can see out of) and mounted by `CarBody.gd`; **Car** in the road atlas switches cars and reopens the drive at the same place. See `docs/cars.md`.
- **Windows** — a Windows build: `scripts/build-windows.sh` cross-compiles the map helper to `atlas.exe`, exports the game with a new "Windows Desktop" preset and packages an NSIS installer (Start menu and desktop shortcuts, an uninstaller and an Apps & features entry, optional removal of the cache) and a portable zip, versioned from `config/version`. The game looks for `atlas.exe` beside itself on Windows. `docs/install.md` covers both systems.
- **Screenshots and playing from source** — README screenshots captured in game by a new `--screenshots` mode (`checks/Screenshots.gd`), which also runs on software Vulkan; `scripts/play.sh` updates a checkout and plays it; `docs/building.md` has a step-by-step setup for a fresh Mac; `build-macos.sh` finds Bun in `~/.bun/bin`.
- **Bridges** — mapped bridges are built as decks with asphalt, edge and centre lines, concrete parapets that keep the car on them, a slab and piers, instead of the car driving down into the creek, valley or rail yard below. One-way decks carry dashed lane lines on the MUTCD 10 ft / 40 ft cycle. Placing the car (a search, or R to recover) and its shadow now stand on a deck instead of the ground beneath it. The helper sets each deck straight between the road benches of its approaches (with a short end fit for approach embankments), follows a bridge across z14 tile edges, remembers its end heights in the cache so every tile it crosses agrees, and keeps the ground under it bare earth. On the Cheyenne I-180 viaduct the test drive had wheel lifts and 1.5 g jolts before and now crosses in both directions with no airtime and at most 0.55 g. `checks/RoadDrive.gd` also reports impacts and its driver lifts off in a slide.
- **Roads** — a smoother driving surface: the 32 × 32 tile height grid produced a slope step at every grid crease in both the visible mesh and the collider, which launched the car; the fix takes the roadbed off the raw grid.
- **Light** — one sun instead of two (the sky panorama's unclipped sun added unshadowed light on top of the `DirectionalLight3D`, giving a shadow-to-sun ratio near 0.5 where photographs show 0.18 to 0.32), longer shadow range, tonemap white point, debanding and anisotropy settings, and the real clock for the sun angle instead of a fixed June afternoon.
- **Immersion** — HUD levels (Off / Glance / Full) with translucent panels instead of opaque bars that were on by default, sound on by default with tyre, wind and gravel layers added to the engine, and a driver head that lags and settles instead of a camera welded to the chassis.
- **Vegetation** — canopies with backlight, occlusion and warm tint instead of opaque dark blobs, near fade and alpha-to-coverage, mown verges and textured swaying field clumps instead of knee-high grass spikes.
- **Open-source project setup** — the repository moved to the `simcruisegame` organization and is public. Added `SECURITY.md` (private vulnerability reporting), `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md` (Contributor Covenant 2.1), issue and pull-request templates, `.editorconfig`, and `scripts/check-helper.sh`, which runs the map helper's 18 offline tests and checks that it bundles. GitHub secret scanning with push protection and Dependabot security alerts are on. The version now lives in `config/version` in `game/project.godot`; the bundle identifier is `io.github.simcruisegame.simcruise`. The README is shorter and the detailed sections moved to `docs/` (install, controls, technical overview, data sources, building, studies, related projects).
- **Random start** — each launch starts on a random road from 36 verified US drives (`game/data/start-pool.json`, OpenStreetMap nodes with the way's bearing), tries three more if one does not load and then the bundled Miami Beach tiles (Ocean Drive and the Flamingo Park blocks), which work offline; **Surprise me** in the search box picks another; check modes (`FieldCheck`, `SeamCheck`, `RoadProfile` among them) start in Miami Beach so their runs repeat.

### Calibration guard

Every change keeps the headless `game/checks/Track.gd` run (`--fixed-fps 60`) inside its bands: 0–60 mph 7.97 ± 0.10 s, 70–0 mph 173 ± 3 ft, 300 ft skidpad 0.89 ± 0.01 g.

# Changelog

Notable changes to SimCruise, newest first. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/) and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/). Dates are release dates, not the survey dates of the data a release uses. The release notes written for Sunday Driver 1.0.0 to 1.0.8 are preserved word for word in [docs/history.md](docs/history.md).

## Unreleased

- **The mirrors show the road behind** (`Presentation/SimCruiseCarMirror`, `SimCruiseCarPresentationComponent`, `Vehicle/SimCruiseVehicle`) — from the driver's seat the rear-view and both door mirrors reflect the street, set the way a driver sets them (the rear-view straight back, the door mirrors a little outward, the passenger's convex); they were flat pale blue. One 768 x 320 capture looks back from the driver's head every third frame: High in the seat 76 to 72 fps, the chase view unchanged (no capture there). `simcruise.Mirrors 0` turns them off.
- **Tiles draw as static meshes** (`World/SimCruiseTileBuilder`, `SimCruiseWorldTile`) — the street tiles' drawing moved from procedural meshes to static meshes built on the worker thread, so their shadows are cached: High in the driver's seat 70 to 76 fps on the target laptop, the chase view 85 to 91; collision is unchanged.
- **City sky glow at night** (`Environment/SimCruiseEnvironment`, `World/SimCruiseWorldStreamer`) — over built-up places the night horizon glows faintly warm from the street lights, so palms and roofs stand out against it; it was black. Open country stays dark.
- **The roadster's gauges light up at night** (`CarPresentation.json`) — the cluster's numerals and ticks and the console labels are backlit like the real panel; at night the dashboard was black.
- **The dashboard clock tells the time** (`Presentation/SimCruiseCarClock`, `CarPresentation.json` `clock`) — the roadster's clock always read 2:15; it now shows the local time at the place you drive (12-hour, US time zones and daylight saving time).
- **Palms are instances of six shared meshes** (`World/SimCruiseTileDetail`, `SimCruiseWorldResources`, `SimCruiseWorldTile`) — about 107 000 fewer triangles in each Miami tile's mesh and collision, and the palms' shaded trunks now take the sky light (they read nearly black before). Frame time unchanged.
- **Faster at High on the target laptop** (`Config/DefaultScalability.ini`, `Environment/SimCruiseEnvironment`, `Gameplay/SimCruiseDriveCapture`) — the drive is now measured (`-SimCruiseDrivePerf`, RTX 2070 Super Max-Q, 1080p): High went from 62 to 70 fps in the driver's seat by marching the cloud shadow map at 192 texels and 12 steps over 20 km instead of 384 and 24 over 50 km (2.1 to 0.56 ms, no visible change).
- **Unreal only** (owner decision, 11 November 2026) — the Godot project (`game/`: scripts, scenes, shaders, `project.godot`, import files, the Godot checks and map overview data) is deleted; it stays at the git tag `godot-final` (`git show godot-final:game/<file>`), and `main` is now the Unreal client. The data the Unreal client reads moved to an engine-neutral `data/`: `data/cars` (car files), `data/art/traffic` (glTF models and manifest), `data/art/ground` (detail photographs), `data/tiles` (the bundled Ocean Drive start and its aerial photographs) and `data/observed` (a helper fixture). Also removed: the Godot build, packaging and check scripts (`scripts/check.sh`, `build-macos.sh`, `build-windows.sh`, `make-dmg.sh`, `play.sh`, the NSIS script) and the Godot, Jolt and font licences. README, controls, technical overview and contributing guide describe the Unreal client; the migration ledger points every Godot path at `godot-final`.
- **The player car is the 2001 roadster (NB2)** (`data/cars/nb2001.json`, default `StartCarId`) — the downloaded model is the 2001-05 facelift, so the car now is one: 142 hp BP-Z3 with sequential valve timing, 2,387 lb; 0-60 mph 7.75 s on the bench against the published 7.8 s. The 1999 car stays as the calibration reference.
- **A drive starts in daylight** — 10:00 local solar time at the place, today; `-SimCruiseRealTime` follows the computer's clock.
- **No tyre brand lettering** (owner decision): confirmed; every car's tyres are drawn as plain rubber.
- **Ocean Drive looks like Ocean Drive** (`World/SimCruiseTileDetail`, `SimCruiseTileBuilder`, `SimCruiseWorldResources`, `SimCruiseWorldStreamer`, `Presentation/SimCruiseLowBeamPattern`, [docs/unreal-architecture.md](docs/unreal-architecture.md#round-2-world-detail-11-november-2026)) — the box buildings in the Miami Beach Art Deco district became pastel stucco hotels with parapets, speed lines, eyebrows, porches with columns, awnings, stepped towers and neon (white condominium towers with balconies above 26 m); coconut palms grow on Lummus Park and South Florida's lawns; street lights stand behind the curbs and really light the road at night, with lit windows, shop interiors and neon following the sun; built-up streets get parking lanes with stall ticks and junctions crosswalks (the car now starts in Ocean Drive's single lane between them); the asphalt is darker, lawns and sand no longer smear the aerial photograph; the sky is a deeper blue with harder light (exposure +0.2 EV, stronger highlight compression, a lower marine aerosol layer); and the low beams have a real cut-off. Before and after: `docs/images/unreal/round2-*`.
- **Automatic shift map follows the pedal** (`Vehicle/SimCruisePowertrainModel`, `SimCruiseVehicleSim`, [docs/cars.md](docs/cars.md#the-unreal-clients-benches)) — the automatic held first gear to 6700 rpm at any pedal. It now changes up early at a light pedal (the roadster 1–2 at about 2550 rpm at 15 %, 3770 rpm at 45 %), runs to the redline only with the pedal floored, kicks down when floored, never hunts, never changes up on the brakes, and holds its gear through corners above 0.3 g. New optional car fields `upshift_rpm_light`, `downshift_rpm_light`, `kickdown_rpm`. Test: `SimCruise.Vehicle.Model.ShiftMap`.
- **Vehicle benches are deterministic again** (`Tests/VehicleBenchWorld`, `VehicleBenches`, `VehicleBenchTests`) — `SimCruise.Vehicle.Convergence.mustang65` failed four runs in five: the bench's test world ran the project's game mode, which streamed the Ocean Drive tiles onto the pad on worker threads, and the skidpad car eventually crashed into them at a moment set by the wall clock. The bench world now runs an empty game mode (and refuses to run anything else), every CarTrack run fails if the car decelerates harder than 5 g, and `SimCruise.Vehicle.Determinism` repeats the whole CarTrack bit for bit, skidpad included.
- **Unreal client: one drive, all the streams together** (`Source/SimCruise/*/Gameplay/SimCruiseGameMode`, `Vehicle/SimCruiseVehicle`, `Config/DefaultGame.ini`, [docs/unreal-architecture.md](docs/unreal-architecture.md#the-drive-integration), [docs/unreal-building.md](docs/unreal-building.md#drive-it)) — launching the game (`UnrealEditor.exe SimCruise.uproject -game`) now starts at the offline Miami Beach start: the nine tiles around Ocean Drive are built from the bundled data (about 15 s), then the 1999 roadster, on Chaos physics with its downloaded model, is placed in the right-hand lane of Ocean Drive facing its travel, in the driver's seat, under the sun and moon where they are in Miami Beach at that moment. The Xbox pad and the keyboard drive it through the driving shell's steering and pedal shaping (the physics applies the shaped rack angle as given), rumble reads the real tyres' slip, the car model's wheels steer, lean, spin and travel from the physics, the brake lamps follow the brake, and recover puts the car back in the nearest road's lane. The world streams around the car and moves it with the floating origin. At night the car has real low beams (two 15 000 cd halogen spot lights from the head-lamp lenses) and the camera adapts to the road they light; before, the street was black. Fixed on the way: the player was never given a car on the empty map (the world drive was decided after the player had logged in); Lumen's cached lighting was clipped under the real sun (`r.EyeAdaptation.CachedLightingPreExposure` 7.5); the turntable got a second sun. `-SimCruiseDriveCapture` photographs the drive (seat, chase, top-down, driving off); `SimCruise.Drive.*` tests the seams, the car on the streamed Ocean Drive and the low beams, and the shell's self-test passes on the real car. Honest state against photographs: the car reads as real; the place does not yet (box buildings, no palms, parked cars or people, a blurred aerial park, a milky sky, pale asphalt, no street lights at night).
- **Unreal client: the cars drive on Chaos** (`Source/SimCruise/{Public,Private}/Vehicle/`, `docs/unreal-architecture.md`) — `ASimCruiseVehicle` is a Chaos rigid body driven by a port of `Powertrain.gd` and `CarSuspension.gd` that runs on the physics thread before every fixed 360 Hz step (`bTickPhysicsAsync`, 1/360 s): ray contacts, springs, dampers, anti-roll bars, bump stops, relaxed slip angles, slip-ratio tyres with combined slip and load sensitivity, engine, clutch, automatic (with the 0.45 m/s reverse inhibitor) and manual gearboxes, differentials, brakes, ABS and traction control where the car had them, aero and rolling resistance, all in SI with one tested conversion boundary. With Godot's own 60 Hz stepping it reproduces Godot's CarTrack to the printed digit for the 1999 roadster; at 360 Hz the results are converged and every car is inside its bands (roadster 7.97 s, 171.9 ft, 0.884 g; Mustang 8.95 s, 263.6 ft, 0.698 g; Corvette 3.46 s against its stated 2.8 s open item, 150.1 ft, 1.024 g), LiftOff matches Godot case for case, and two identical runs are bit-identical. The port fixed two step-size effects (the clutch's coupling cap now uses the calibration substep; Godot's 60 Hz roll oscillation in the Corvette's launch is gone) and an anti-roll bar helper that returned 0 for thin bars (the roadster's rear). The Mustang's estimated drum torques are scaled by 0.975 in an Unreal calibration overlay (`Content/Vehicle/Calibration/`) to keep its 70–0 at Car Life's 0.62 g. The driving shell gets throttle, brake, steer (thumbstick-shaped), handbrake, clutch, shift and manual controls and per-wheel telemetry for HUD, rumble and audio. The vehicle survives its body's physics state being recreated (it used to keep stepping the freed particle and crash) and keeps the car's mass properties when the game thread recomputes the body's. 25 automation tests (`SimCruise.Vehicle.*`; `docs/unreal-building.md`).
- **Unreal: drive on the real place** (`Source/SimCruise/{Public,Private}/{Atlas,World}/`, `docs/unreal-architecture.md`) — the Unreal client streams the bundled Miami Beach tiles with no network, and other places through the atlas helper, which it runs as an asynchronous, bounded, cancellable process with typed errors. A 3 x 3 ring of tiles is built on worker threads in a local frame with a floating origin; results of an older place or of a tile left behind are rejected. Roads are engineered ribbons ported from the Godot client's road-edge work: filleted and smoothed centrelines with straight, continuous edges, a fitted grade, crown and superelevation, curb, gutter and sidewalk where a sidewalk is mapped, junctions joined into one surface, turnaround pads, MUTCD-style markings and collision the car drives on. Seam contracts make both tiles of a seam build the same section, so terrain, road heights and road edges meet to the millimetre across all 24 bundled tiles. Terrain carries the aerial photograph with land-cover detail, the sea and lakes have water, and mapped buildings stand as massing in the Godot client's general US paint and brick colours. A tile is reported ready only once its collision is in the physics scene. Eleven automation tests (`SimCruise.Atlas.*`, `SimCruise.World.*`) cover the helper client, tile math, offline streaming, seams, collision under every road vertex, edge zig-zags, stale jobs, rebasing a live streamer and deterministic builds.
- **Unreal client: the cars look like the real ones, down to the parts** (`Source/SimCruise/*/Presentation`, `Content/Vehicles/CarPresentation.json`, [docs/unreal-architecture.md](docs/unreal-architecture.md)). The downloaded models of the 1999 roadster (niev's NB convertible with interior, CC BY 4.0, now in `game/art/traffic/miata_nb-5d103c56`), the 1965 Mustang and the Corvette C8 load at run time from their glTF files and are fitted to each car's data: wheelbase, published track, static ride height, real tyre centres. Wheels spin, steer and ride on knuckles with their brake calipers; the cabin steering wheel (found on the driver's side, left- or right-hand drive) turns at each car's published steering ratio (15:1, 21.7:1, 15.7:1); brake and tail lamps light on all three cars (the Mustang's lenses sit in its body's texture atlas and are given the lamp role by profile boxes), while white lenses behind the axles stay unlit as reversing lamps. Maker badges, plate lettering and centre-cap logos are found and hidden by name, by shape and by profile box (the NB's `MX-5` and `mazda` tail lettering). The NB model is not added to the Godot traffic manifest, so the Godot client is unchanged. Every surface gets a physical material role: clear-coat paint with metallic flakes under the coat and orange peel on it (the NB in Emerald Mica, the C8 in Torch Red), tinted laminated glass, rubber with grain and road dust, cast-iron discs with lathe marks, grained plastics, leather, cloth and carpet. The NB's 1.1 million-triangle body draws with Nanite. A turntable (`SimCruiseTurntableGameMode`, `SimCruise.Turntable`) photographs each car from eight angles plus the driver's seat, a wheel, steering and braking, at the highest settings; `SimCruise.Presentation.*` automation tests check the fit of every car.

- **Unreal client: light, sky and rendering** (`Source/SimCruise/*/Environment/`, `Config/DefaultEngine.ini`, `Config/DefaultScalability.ini`, [docs/unreal-architecture.md](docs/unreal-architecture.md#light-sky-and-rendering)) — every game world gets one sky built in C++ at run time: the sun and the moon placed from the real latitude, longitude and UTC time (NOAA solar calculator, reproducing NREL's SPA reference case to 0.03°; Meeus for the moon and its phase), in physical units (127.8 klx sun above the air, Allen's phase law for moonlight), a physically based atmosphere whose aerosol haze follows the visibility (Koschmieder), volumetric clouds whose cover follows the METAR sky cover from `helper/conditions.mjs`, mist below 8 km, a real-time sky light, and road wetness that rises in rain and dries over 15 minutes. The camera exposes in EV100 inside a band taken from Ocean Drive photographs (1.1–1.6 EV below a grey-card reading), shows night darker than day and loses colour in moonlight. Lumen GI and reflections, virtual shadow maps and TSR; Low / Medium / High / Ultra presets with an auto-pick (the RTX 2070 Super Max-Q starts on High). Measured at 1080p on that GPU in the new lighting lab street (`-SimCruiseLab`): Low 225, Medium 141, High 95–99 and Ultra 72 fps at a clear noon, 198 / 123 / 88 / 65 fps under broken cloud; Ultra was 52 fps until the cloud-shadow march (6.0 ms) and TSR's 200 % history (4.0 ms) were budgeted. 12 automation tests (`SimCruise.Environment.*`). A streamed level or World Partition cell that brings its own sun or sky has it turned off as it arrives; the graphics auto-pick leaves Play-In-Editor and renderer-less runs alone. Against Commons photographs of Ocean Drive the sunlit ground is close (0.3–0.5 EV bright); the sky is paler and the afternoon shade darker and bluer than in the photographs (open).
- **Unreal client: a drivable shell with the Xbox pad** (`Source/SimCruise/{Public,Private}/Gameplay/`, `Config/DefaultInput.ini`, `Config/DefaultEngine.ini`) — the input, cameras, HUD and rumble around the car, ported from the Godot client and built in C++ with no binary assets. Enhanced Input actions and the mapping context are created in code from one table that matches [docs/controls.md](docs/controls.md) (triggers, left stick, Y camera, B manual, RB / X shift, LB clutch, A parking brake, View recover, Menu atlas, L3 HUD; W/S/A/D, Space, H, T, E/Q, C, R, F2, F3, F6, M, L, Tab, /). Steering shaping is driver-side only and follows the audited pad-steering plan: Godot's speed-aware range and stick map, a rack state slewed in rad/s, and a countersteer allowance toward the front axle's velocity angle (up to |β_f| + 5°, at up to 40°/s) so a 15° slide at 45 mph can be caught, while releasing the stick still returns to exactly zero; key pedals ramp like a foot. The driver camera ports `Cockpit.gd`'s head model (lagged g lean, brake dip, gaze lead, 70 % horizon hold) at the car's `driver_eye` through the natural 45° lens; the chase camera swings with the body slip angle so a slide reads on screen; a canvas HUD has Off, Glance and Full levels with a g-g meter ringed by the car's published skidpad and braking limits; pad rumble ports the slip-haptics branch (front scrub, rear slip and wheelspin, 8 Hz ABS pulse, road texture) and adds the impulse triggers under GameInput. The vehicle plugs in through the small `ISimCruiseVehicleControl` interface; until it does, a clearly labelled placeholder single-track car drives. 13 `SimCruise.Gameplay.*` automation tests and an in-game `-SimCruiseShellSelfTest` (exit 0 / 1) pass; see [docs/unreal-architecture.md](docs/unreal-architecture.md#driving-shell). Review fixes: the head model and g-meter differentiate velocity over the real frame (a clamped step misread the lean above 240 or below 20 fps); the chase camera's ground probe and recover/reset start near the car, so an overpass deck cannot capture them; a stopped car no longer rumbles from slip angles left over from a slide; the pawn's tick prerequisite and the rumble are dropped on unpossess. Rumble has not yet been felt on a physical pad, and plan 04's slide-catch gate for the countersteer allowance is not met on the placeholder car (a full-lock catch overcorrects into a spin that the capped rack avoids); it has to be run on the real vehicle.

- **Your own model for a car** (`Drive.gd`, `ModelRig.gd`, `CarBody.gd`) — a binary glTF at `user://cars/<car id>.glb` (for example a model you bought) replaces that car's exterior on your machine only: it is read at run time, turned upright if its file is not Y-up, fitted to the car's wheelbase and ride height, and never enters the repository. `--stock-model` ignores it. - **Road pipeline gaps from the field test** (`helper/road-widths.mjs`, `helper/road-grade.mjs`, `helper/usgs-ground.mjs`, `RoadSurface.gd`) — four fixes for problems D and E of `docs/research/field-test-2026-11-10.md`. *One-way widths:* a one-way carriageway (half of a divided highway, one street of a couplet) is two 3.6 m lanes plus an outside and a median shoulder by class instead of the full two-way class width (trunk 10.5 m instead of 16 m, motorway 11.4, primary 9.6, secondary 9.3, tertiary 9.0), and a ramp is one lane with ramp shoulders (6 m; ramps used to take their motorway's 19 m). Two-way roads keep their class widths. The width comes from the vector tile's class, one-way and ramp flags alone, so both tiles of every seam agree: a draft that read OSM lane counts per zoom-14 tile from Esri's replica gave a tile built during a failed query other widths than its neighbours, and on the Bozeman tiles South Willson Avenue already changed width (10.2 → 13.8 m) at a zoom-14 boundary, where seam chaining then stopped. *Survey data stays valid:* the road signature that bundled survey data is keyed on (`SurveyOutcropGround.gd` `road_signature()`, `road-layout.mjs` `laneRoadSignature()`, `lab/road-crossfall/prepare.py`) now signs each road's geometry and the reference width of its class instead of its own width, and the crossfall half-width test uses that reference width. Every own width is at most the reference width, so a survey made for the reference width still covers the paved road. With the lane-count draft, all five survey-keyed tiles lost their data without an error (the three lidar crossfall registry tiles and both Needles outcrop tiles); now all five match, with signatures identical to main's, `OutcropCheck` passes on both Needles tiles (road height error 0.0, and it now also checks that a moved vertex or a class change drops the data while a narrower width keeps it), `CrossfallMeshCheck` builds 2 runs on Anchorage 5479-18579 and the Anchorage K Street `--crossfall-drive` passes (718/718 four-wheel contact, 0.04 mm worst ray). `RoadSurface.gd` keeps each station's half width (`halves`) in `lines` instead of the chain-wide maximum; `GuardRails.gd` already placed rails from each station's own shoulder edge. Cache eviction now manages the draft's `lane-widths-v1-*` files and removes `.source-state/elevation-retry-*` records older than a day. *Width per station:* `road_surface` rows carry the paved width at each station, so a chain that joins a one-way carriageway to a wider two-way road narrows over 20 m at the joint instead of taking the widest width along its whole length (each part is also benched to its own width). *Superelevation run-out:* where two banked roads overlap and their cross slopes disagree (a couplet splitting from its approach, a ramp joining), the bank runs out over 20 m past where their footprints meet, as at a real at-grade junction, so one edge no longer stands above the other (Eureka CA, US-101: 0.35 m). *Tile edges:* a road is graded when any of its segments crosses the tile or its apron, not only when a vertex lies inside it, so a straight road crossing a tile edge to edge is no longer dropped from `road_surface` (Leakey TX, RR 337: 525 m of road on the raw grid; 846 m in Eureka and 2.7 km in Bozeman on the same check). *Elevation:* a tile whose 3DEP read fails for a transient reason is rejected (the helper exits 1 and the game retries in 15 s) instead of being built on Terrarium beside 3DEP neighbours; after 3 rejections in 20 minutes it is built on Terrarium after all, with `road_grade.elevation_fallback` saying why. A simulated outage on a Bozeman tile left a 1.93 m seam with the old helper (RoadDrive: a 19.9 → 5.6 m/s impact on North Bozeman Avenue, a rollover on North Willson Avenue). RoadDrive at 0.4 g, Miata, forward plus reverse, tiles built by main's helper and by this one from the same cache, main → now: Vidalia GA 1st Street (US-280) air events 8 → 0, jolts above 0.5 g 116 → 0, above 1 g 15 → 0; Eureka CA Broadway (US-101) air 6 → 1, jolts above 0.5 g 35 → 0, above 1 g 14 → 0, one impact → none, worst vertical 10.3 g → 0.23 g (the driver still strays 10.3 m from its route at the couplet split at 4 m/s, as with the lane-count draft, which counted that inside a 22.8 m road; this one counts one off-road event against the 10.5 m carriageway); Leakey TX RR 337 air 19 → 0, jolts above 0.5 g 265 → 0, above 1 g 64 → 0; Bozeman MT West Main jolts above 0.5 g 4 → 0, North 7th Avenue clean on both. These match the lane-count draft's drives event for event. RoadProfile creases per km (ground collider, primary to trunk): Vidalia 13.2 → 11.3, Eureka 21.1 → 14.9, Bozeman 12.2 → 7.1, Leakey 15.9 → 0.0 (lane-count draft 11.1, 15.6, 6.0, 0.0). `lab/check-road-pipeline.mjs` (in `scripts/check-helper.sh`) covers the widths, the signature, edge-to-edge segments, the elevation retry and the eviction of retry records offline.
- **Road markings from the mapped lane counts** (`LaneMarkings.gd`, `helper/lane-counts.mjs`) — the field test of 2026-11-10 found Moab's five-lane Main Street (US-191) drawn as a two-lane road with a double yellow line, Kearney's US-30 with no markings, and edge lines missing almost everywhere, because every marking was guessed from the road class. The helper now reads the OpenStreetMap `lanes`, `lanes:forward`, `lanes:backward`, `oneway` and `maxspeed` tags of the major roads around each tile from Esri's OSM North America highways replica (one query per 2 × 2 tile group, cached 28 days; `--without-lane-counts` turns it off) and matches them to every segment of the tile's roads (tile JSON `road_lanes`, matched only to parallel ways within 6 m: two-way to two-way, and one-way only to a one-way way running the same direction, so a one-way street never takes the opposite carriageway or a side street's mouth; by majority vote along the segment). The query is off the first tile's critical path: the tile command answers from the cache at once (`road_lanes` status `pending` when nothing is cached), and `BuildingStream.gd` fetches the counts in the background for every streamed tile still waiting for them, nearest first (the ring around the car, so a ring tile, built at least a tile away, is repainted before its lines come into view; one request serves a 2 × 2 group and the rest of the group reads the cache) (helper `lane-counts` command, road rows passed in a cache file) and repaints the markings; `--wait-lane-counts` makes the tile command wait, for tools and field tests. The game paints them after MUTCD Part 3: white edge lines (6 in on arterials) on motorways, trunks and primaries everywhere and on collectors outside towns; a yellow left edge on one-way carriageways; a double yellow centre line on two-way roads with two or more lanes each way or in town; on rural two-lane roads a broken yellow line, solid on the side without the Table 3B-1 passing sight distance (measured over the road surface with a 3.5 ft eye and object, zones under 400 ft removed); a two-way left-turn lane (solid outside, broken inside) only where OSM counts a lane in neither direction (`lanes` = forward + backward + 1, OSM's `lanes:both_ways`, e.g. 5 = 2 + 1 + 2) or gives an odd total with no split, and, in town, for a 2 + 1 or 3 + 2 split (the left-turn pocket of a junction approach). Outside towns such a split stays as mapped: a climbing or passing lane, with the two-lane side solid and the single lane broken where it has the passing sight distance; 10 ft white lane lines on a world-anchored 40 ft cycle between lanes in the same direction; no centre line on a single lane or under 16 ft; nothing on local streets. Without a mapped count a segment takes the counts of the same road within 250 m, then the counts mapped on most of the same street in the tile (symmetric splits only), then the US default: one lane each way, and on a one-way road the lanes its paved width was built for by `helper/road-widths.mjs` (two on a one-way carriageway, one on a ramp), so paint and surface agree; the width is the ribbon's own paved width at each station (`RoadSurface.gd` `halves`). Lanes are never narrower than 2.75 m: a count the road cannot hold drops the centre lane, then lanes from the busier direction. Town or rural (edge lines on collectors, passing zones) follows a mapped speed limit, else the share of the street's length in the tile that is built up, with a majority over 150 m each way on mixed streets, so it no longer switches from block to block; the built-up count is scaled up near the tile edge. Lines follow the road ribbon's 2 m stations and sit 4.8 cm above the surface the tyres drive on (the ribbon's own cross-sections, or `height_at()` where ribbons overlap or there is none); a quad ends where the surface leaves its chord by 1.5 cm (crests and sags), a step between two points is halved down to 25 cm and cut out if it is a real step, a piece across a step in the road surface is left out, and each tile paints only inside its own square. They stop where a crossing road runs through, and give way to measured paint (`PaintEvidence`, `MeasuredSection`, `RoadLayout`, `RoadSurvey`). `checks/LaneMarkingCheck.gd` (default suite) tests the rules on fixed cross-sections and a stripe over a synthetic 8.9° crest and a 0.5 m step, paints two bundled tiles again on a synthetic road ribbon (crests, superelevation, a width taper, overlapping ribbons at every junction and a mapped 2 + TWLTL + 2 count; the bundled tiles alone have no ribbon), checks that the lane counts are fetched for the whole ring nearest first, and fails on any script error raised while painting (`checks/ScriptErrorGuard.gd`, a `Logger`: a runtime error only abandons one road, so the check used to print PASSED over one), then builds tiles and samples every stripe triangle (corners, edge midpoints, centre) against the tile's colliders: the paint must sit 1–10 cm above the ribbon trimesh (or the nearest collider off it); places where the ground or a kerb collider rises above the ribbon, or the road surface itself folds more than 3 cm inside one piece, are counted as road-surface defects. With `--dir` it also reports marking time, height lookups and town/rural changes per tile; `lab/check-lane-counts.mjs` (helper suite) tests the tag parsing, matching (including one-way direction) and the cache-only read offline, and that every one-way width of `road-widths.mjs` is painted with two lanes and every ramp with one. Every ribbon is painted, whatever its class: `road-grade.mjs` chains a local street into the major road it continues as and names the chain after its first piece, and those major roads used to be painted along the map line beside their ribbon; each station is matched to its road by its own paved width. Marking cost, measured with `LaneMarkingCheck --dir` against the same tree with only the per-station width fix, three alternating runs each on a shared machine: Salida CO (9 tiles) 16.6 / 19.0 / 14.8 % of tile build → 7.8 / 7.5 / 7.3 % (a later pair under heavier load from other jobs: 16.6 → 9.5 % and 14.8 → 7.6 %; marking time 2.2–4.4× lower in every pair), the US-50 tile at Monarch Pass 52 → 26 ms (20 → 12 %); sparse single tiles with many lines still reach 12–18 %. The time went to height lookups where another ribbon was assumed near (now tested against the bounding boxes of the other ribbons' quads), quads drawn vertex by vertex (now one triangle fan), corner height lookups on a single ribbon (now flat across the 15 cm stripe), and stations on straight, uniform stretches, which are now left out where every line stays within 5 mm of its chord. `RoadSurface.height()` rejects quads by their bounding box first. `checks/RailView.gd --rail-car` frames the car and adds a driver's-eye `road-ahead.jpg` and a high `road-above.jpg`.

Four new checks joined the default suite today: `LiftOff`, `TractionControlCheck`, `CombinedSlip` and `AbsReverse` (see [docs/cars.md](docs/cars.md)).

Every car did it (108–1,186 ticks on one Dragon run); the light rear-drive Miata spun. `Powertrain.gd` now changes direction only below 0.45 m/s, like a real selector's reverse inhibitor, and a pedal too light to brake coasts. See `docs/cars.md`.
- **Tyres that peak and slide** (`Powertrain.gd`, `CarSpec.gd`) — longitudinal grip now follows a slip-ratio curve: full grip to the peak slip (0.10), falling toward a locked or spinning wheel by `slip_ratio_falloff`, so a spinning tyre also loses side grip; side-force relaxation length grows with load. The five modern cars (radials, ABS) fall by 10 % (Wong's 83–94 % sliding/peak on dry asphalt); the 1999 roadster and the 1965 Mustang stay flat as before, with their original parameters (the roadster's 0.963 peak and 1.105 lateral scale: a 1.07 peak sliding to 0.963 had raised its trail-braking grip 5.3 % without a measurement; the Mustang's estimated bias-ply curve made its full-throttle 0–60 10.7 s against 8.9 s). ABS (`abs_enabled`, renamed from `abs`, which shadowed the built-in) modulates each wheel about its peak slip in either direction of travel. Traction control holds the most-slipping driven wheel at the same peak slip: the slip is read through a 20 ms filter and a slew limit, proportional 0.15 plus integral 3/s, no wind-up while the clutch slips at capacity, held through gear changes, torque cut off within 50 ms and restored over 0.15 s. A low-rate director gives each car a persona — commuter, delivery van (drawn as a pickup), cautious elder, hurried driver, tourist — with its own IDM parameters varied ±10 % per driver, and about once a second plans its route through the next junctions (turn choice by persona: bigger roads and straight on for commuters and hurried drivers, residential streets for vans, wandering tourists), the cornering speed to slow to before a sharp turn, stops at mapped signals (SignalControls matches and OSM signal nodes, fixed-time two-phase plans with a yellow dilemma decided by comfortable braking) and the occasional kerbside stop of a delivery van, which following cars ease round. ## SimCruise 1.1.0 (2026-11-09)

Renamed from Sunday Driver. The work below follows the 8 November 2026 design-panel report kept as [docs/roadmap.md](docs/roadmap.md).

### Changes

- **Controls** — speed-aware cap on keyboard and pad steering (the rack still allowed 18.8° at 45 mph where about 3° is usable), slower pedal and steering ramps, a keyboard-tap bench, and one-press recovery that notices a roof-down or stuck car, prompts for R and searches every road, not only main roads.
- **Mountain roads** — streamed roads are now built as road benches: a smooth profile measured along the curving road, a flat shelf with superelevation in rural curves, and cut and fill slopes that meet the hillside, instead of a terrain grid that skipped steep side slopes and capped corrections at 1.5 m. Streamed tiles use a 128 grid drawn adaptively, so only cells on the bench gain triangles. On the first 7.7 km of the Tail of the Dragon (US 129, Deals Gap) the RMS slope change under the tyres fell from 1.19° to 0.20° and the worst from 21.6°/m to 3.3°/m; the scripted test drive (`checks/RoadDrive.gd`, new) rolled the car within 0.7 km before and now finishes both directions without leaving the ground. `checks/RoadProfile.gd` gains `--dir=` and `--classes=` to measure any streamed tiles.

### Calibration guard

Every change keeps the headless `game/checks/Track.gd` run (`--fixed-fps 60`) inside its bands: 0–60 mph 7.97 ± 0.10 s, 70–0 mph 173 ± 3 ft, 300 ft skidpad 0.89 ± 0.01 g. Baseline on the 1.0.8 code: 7.948 s, 173.0 ft, 0.885 g. `RideCheck.gd`, `TurningCircle.gd` (30.35 ft left, 30.58 ft right at 1.0 and 1.8 m/s against the 30.2 ft specification) and `Wall.gd` (70 mph into a 15 cm wall, not crossed) also pass on that code.

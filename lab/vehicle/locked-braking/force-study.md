# 1999 US Sport brake and steering transient

At 60 km/h on a level, dry surface, both front wheels were stationary in 50 consecutive samples. With the rack at +20.48°, the existing model produced 1,182 N to the right. Its force pointed 13.50° away from the actual contact-patch slide direction. A −20.48° rack input mirrored the result. The patch reduces the lateral error below 0.001 N in both directions. Both implementations use identical car geometry, braking torque, tire loads and the 60 Hz Jolt integrator.

In a separate free-chassis trial the car entered at 50 mph. Full service braking began at zero seconds, then steering started 0.23 s later. The original car rotated 71.74° by the time it slowed below 1 m/s. The revised car rotated 0.33°, with four contacts throughout. Straight braking covered 27.67 m in both builds. The left and right trials behaved symmetrically. The figures come from headless Godot telemetry.

The original force calculation compressed a rolling lateral slip-angle model and a separate wheel-longitudinal braking demand into a friction ellipse. Once a tire stops rolling, the tread is sliding along the road with the contact point. A force computed principally in the steered wheel’s axial direction can generate a false chassis yaw moment. The revision directs the saturated reaction against tread sliding velocity, retains the existing force ellipse and feeds the same longitudinal component back into the six-substep rotational dynamics. The final 20% of wheel rolling speed blends the two regimes continuously. That threshold is a numerical transition, estimated for this model. Speeds below 1.5 m/s retain the established low-speed contact behavior. The general locked-wheel relation is described in Chalmers’ vehicle dynamics compendium and the university’s brake-control research. Project Chrono also documents a coupled longitudinal/lateral Fiala tire. [Chalmers Vehicle Dynamics Compendium](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/520229/file/520229_Fulltext.pdf) · [Brake Control for Lateral Vehicle Dynamics](https://odr.chalmers.se/server/api/core/bitstreams/1942a68e-e4a4-47e1-952f-ec6df3e92d1e/content) · [Chrono tire formulations](https://api.projectchrono.org/development/wheeled_tire.html).

The ordinary unbraked 40 mph steering pulse, lateral relaxation step, steady skidpad, torque-bias/split-grip test and parking circle reproduced the previous telemetry. Straight 0–60 mph remained 7.9481 s, 70–0 mph was 172.9993 ft before and 172.9992 ft after, and the demonstrated dry skidpad figure remained 0.88464 g. The original rear Hard S shock-dynamometer regression passed. The front damper, true OE tire relaxation characteristics and rear toe-through-bump still lack individual-car measurements. Mazda’s workshop toe target agrees with 0.15° per wheel. The alignment tables specify desired camber at ride height and do not supply a rear toe-versus-travel curve. The modeled car runs without an ABS controller. The bench tests chassis behavior during actual lock-up and does not establish ordinary-road tire feel or measure any individual car. [Mazda workshop manual, sections 02–11–1 and 02–11–3](https://www.mellens.net/mazda/Mazda-Miata-1999-2001.pdf) · [Fat Cat Motorsports’ comparative rear shock test](https://www.fatcatmotorsports.com/igallery/graphics/OE_NB_Showa_and_HardS_vs_NA_OE_Rpkg_and_aftermarket_HD1.GIF).
